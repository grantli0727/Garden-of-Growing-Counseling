Journal/On growing

What change actually looks like

A short read from the practice · 3 min

Gardens make a tempting metaphor, and they also lie a little. Bloom is the part people photograph. Most of a plant’s life is root, weather, and waiting. Therapy is closer to that than to a before-and-after.

Change, when it is real, is often plain. You pause for half a second before the old reaction. You tell the truth a day sooner than you used to. You rest without first earning it. You notice you are angry and do not turn it into a case against yourself. None of this looks like a glow. It looks like an ordinary week with one different choice in it.

Plateaus belong here too. A stretch where nothing seems to move is not proof that the work failed. Sometimes the system is integrating. Sometimes you are tired. Sometimes the next honest thing is to say, out loud, that you feel stuck, and to let that be the session.

You do not have to bloom on anyone else’s timeline, including the one you invented when you decided to get help. Growing can include grief for the years that were harder than they needed to be. It can include relief that is quiet enough to miss if you are only watching for fireworks.

If you want company for that kind of change, the practice is here. If you are in crisis, call or text 988, or call 911. This page is a reflection, not care in itself.
