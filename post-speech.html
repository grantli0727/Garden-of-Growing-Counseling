Journal/Starting out

You do not need a speech prepared

A short read from the practice · 4 min

A lot of people delay the first session because they think they owe the hour a coherent story. They rehearse in the car. They worry they will cry too soon, or not at all, or forget the thing that actually matters. They imagine a therapist waiting with a clipboard and a correct answer they have failed to study for.

That is not the room. The first hour is a beginning, and beginnings are allowed to be clumsy. “I don’t know where to start” is a complete opening. So is “something feels off and I am tired of managing it alone.” So is silence, for a minute, while you find the sentence that is true.

What the hour is for, at the start, is simpler than a diagnosis or a plan. It is a chance to see whether you can sit with another person and tell a little of the truth. It is a chance for the practice to be honest about whether this is the right fit. Fees, pace, and what you hope might change are part of that conversation. None of it requires a performance.

If you have been carrying the story by yourself, you may not have language for it yet. That is ordinary. Language shows up when there is somewhere safe to put it. You can arrive with a knot instead of a narrative.

Reading this is not the same as being in the room. If you are in crisis, call or text 988, or call 911. If you are simply tired of waiting until you sound composed, you can begin before you feel ready.
